How is Malaysian overtime calculated?

For a monthly-rated employee, the Ordinary Rate of Pay (ORP) is commonly calculated by dividing the monthly salary by 26. The Hourly Rate of Pay (HRP) is then calculated by dividing the ORP by the employee's normal working hours per day.

Type of Work OT Rate
Normal Working Day 1.5×
Rest Day 2.0×
Public Holiday 3.0×

Example calculation

Suppose your monthly salary is RM3,000 and your normal working hours are 8 hours per day.

Ordinary Rate of Pay: RM3,000 ÷ 26 = RM115.38 per day.

Hourly Rate: RM115.38 ÷ 8 = RM14.42 per hour.

Therefore, 10 hours of normal-day OT would be approximately:

RM14.42 × 1.5 × 10 = RM216.35

Who is covered?

The Employment Act 1955 provides statutory protection to employees in Peninsular Malaysia and the Federal Territory of Labuan, subject to the applicable provisions.

Employees earning more than RM4,000 per month generally do not receive the statutory overtime, rest-day and public-holiday payment provisions unless they fall within the applicable manual-worker category.

Sabah and Sarawak have separate labour ordinances, so this calculator should not automatically be treated as the statutory calculation for those states.

Important information

Please note: This calculator provides an estimate for informational purposes. Actual overtime payments may depend on your employment contract, employee category, working arrangement and applicable Malaysian labour legislation.

Information is based on guidance from the Jabatan Tenaga Kerja Semenanjung Malaysia (JTKSM) and the Employment Act 1955. Labour rules and interpretations may change. For important payroll matters, verify the calculation with your employer or the relevant labour authority.

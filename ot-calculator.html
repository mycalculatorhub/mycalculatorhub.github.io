How is Malaysian overtime calculated?

For a monthly-rated employee, the ordinary rate of pay (ORP) is commonly calculated by dividing the monthly salary by 26. The hourly rate of pay is then calculated by dividing the ordinary rate of pay by the employee's normal working hours per day.

The statutory overtime multipliers used by this calculator are:

  • Normal working day: 1.5× hourly rate
  • Rest day: 2.0× hourly rate
  • Public holiday: 3.0× hourly rate

Example calculation

Suppose your monthly salary is RM3,000 and your normal working hours are 8 hours per day.

Ordinary rate of pay: RM3,000 ÷ 26 = approximately RM115.38 per day.

Hourly rate: RM115.38 ÷ 8 = approximately RM14.42 per hour.

Therefore, 10 hours of normal-day OT would be approximately: RM14.42 × 1.5 × 10 = RM216.35.

Who is covered?

The Employment Act 1955 amendments provide protection to private sector employees in Peninsular Malaysia and the Federal Territory of Labuan regardless of salary, but employees earning more than RM4,000 per month generally do not receive the statutory OT, rest-day and public-holiday payment provisions unless they fall within the applicable manual-worker category.

Sabah and Sarawak have separate labour ordinances, so this calculator should not automatically be treated as the statutory calculation for those states.
